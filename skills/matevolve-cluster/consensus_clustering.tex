\section{Agentic consensus clustering of structural motifs}
\label{sec:cluster}

We partition the $N$ motif descriptions into $K$ groups of similar geometry with
an agentic analogue of Lloyd's $k$-means algorithm in which the metric space is
replaced by a large language model (LLM). Each motif $m_i$ is a natural-language
description rather than a feature vector, and each cluster centroid $p_k$ is a
short textual \emph{prototype} that states the geometry its members share. The
algorithm keeps the two alternating steps of $k$-means---assignment and
centroid update---and adds a model-adaptation step so that the number of clusters
$K$ is discovered rather than fixed (Table~\ref{tab:kmeans}).

Crucially, the LLM is used only as a \emph{qualitative} operator: each worker
casts a single categorical vote (the prototype a motif best matches, or
``none''), and never produces or compares numbers. All quantitative
quantities---per-motif consensus, inter-cluster confusion, co-association, and
the convergence statistics---are computed by a deterministic routine from the
vote logs. This assigns to the LLM the judgement it performs well (geometric
similarity of free text) and to ordinary code the counting it performs exactly,
and it makes every merge, split, and stopping decision reproducible. The scheme
is a form of consensus / evidence-accumulation clustering, with the stochastic
LLM assignment supplying the base partitions.

\subsection{Ensemble assignment and consensus}

Let prototypes be $P=\{p_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ and let each motif $m_i$ be assigned $R$
times across randomized, overlapping batches, giving labels
$a_r(i)\in\{1,\dots,K,\varnothing\}$ for $r=1,\dots,R$ ($\varnothing$ = ``none'').
With vote counts $n_{i,k}=\lvert\{r:a_r(i)=k\}\rvert$, the dominant assignment and
its consensus are
\begin{equation}
d(i)=\arg\max_{k} n_{i,k},
\qquad
s_i=\frac{n_{i,d(i)}}{R}\in(0,1].
\label{eq:assign}
\end{equation}
A motif is \emph{confident} when $s_i\ge s^\star$ and \emph{contested} otherwise;
a motif with $d(i)=\varnothing$ is an outlier. Equation~\eqref{eq:assign} is the
ensemble counterpart of the $k$-means assignment step, with low $s_i$ playing the
role of a large distance to the nearest centroid.

\subsection{Centroid update}

Writing $C_k=\{i:d(i)=k\}$, the prototype is recomputed by an LLM summarizer
$\Gamma$ that reads (a sample of) the members and re-states their shared geometry,
\begin{equation}
p_k \leftarrow \Gamma\!\left(\{\,m_i : i\in C_k\,\}\right),
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
the textual analogue of the arithmetic mean $\mu_k=\frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{i\in
C_k}x_i$.

\subsection{Discovering $K$: birth, merge, split}

Unlike $k$-means, $K$ adapts. Recurring outliers seed new prototypes
(\emph{birth}). Redundant and over-broad clusters are detected directly from the
vote log. The \emph{confusion} between two clusters,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{confuse}(A,B)=\bigl|\{\,i:\ n_{i,A}>0 \ \wedge\ n_{i,B}>0\,\}\bigr|,
\qquad
\varphi(A,B)=\frac{\mathrm{confuse}(A,B)}{\min(|C_A|,|C_B|)},
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
counts motifs voted into \emph{both} $A$ and $B$; when $\varphi(A,B)\ge
\varphi_{\mathrm{merge}}$ the two prototypes are indistinguishable to the model
and are \emph{merged}. A cluster is a \emph{split} candidate when its contested
fraction $q_A=|\{i\in C_A:s_i<s^\star\}|/|C_A|$ exceeds $q_{\mathrm{split}}$;
whether it splits or merges is decided by where the stray votes point. If they
concentrate on a single rival the cluster is merged with it; otherwise the
\emph{co-association}
\begin{equation}
C_{ij}=\frac{1}{R}\bigl|\{\,r:\ a_r(i)=a_r(j)\ne\varnothing\,\}\bigr|
\label{eq:coassoc}
\end{equation}
is thresholded ($C_{ij}\ge c_{\mathrm{th}}$) and its connected components define
the sub-clusters along which $C_A$ is split. A just-split pair is barred from
re-merging for one round (hysteresis), which suppresses split--merge oscillation.

\subsection{Convergence}

Two statistics govern termination: the mean consensus
$\bar s_t=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i s_i$ (a partition-quality gate) and the
label-invariant partition change
\begin{equation}
\rho_t = 1-\mathrm{ARI}\!\left(d_t,\,d_{t-1}\right),
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{ARI}$ is the Adjusted Rand Index between successive rounds'
assignments (invariant to the relabeling induced by merge/split). Because the
prototype is re-written by the \emph{stochastic} operator $\Gamma$ at each
M-step, assignment stability ($\rho_t\!\to\!0$) does not by itself imply that the
centroids have settled---unlike Euclidean $k$-means, where the centroid is a
deterministic mean of its members. We therefore add a \emph{prototype-stability}
condition, the textual analogue of the centroid-shift test
$\lVert\mu^{t}-\mu^{t-1}\rVert<\mathrm{tol}$. Rather than thresholding a numeric
distance between texts, we let the model judge it: after the M-step, an LLM
compares each cluster's previous and updated prototype and returns
$\sigma_k^{(t)}=1$ if they describe the \emph{same} archetype under the resolution
rule (pure wording changes excluded) and $0$ otherwise. The round's stability is
the fraction
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_t=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_k^{(t)},
\label{eq:protostab}
\end{equation}
and the round is prototype-stable when no birth/merge/split occurred and
$\Sigma_t\ge\sigma_{\min}$. As an optimization, a cluster whose membership is
unchanged (Jaccard $J_k=\lvert C_k^{(t)}\cap C_k^{(t-1)}\rvert/\lvert
C_k^{(t)}\cup C_k^{(t-1)}\rvert\ge J_{\mathrm{freeze}}$) is taken as stable without
a comparison and need not be re-synthesized. The algorithm stops when $\bar
s_t\ge s_{\min}$, $\rho_t<\varepsilon$, and prototype stability all hold for two
consecutive rounds, or at a round cap. Because $\bar s_t$ enters the test, a
partition that is merely stationary but internally inconsistent (low consensus)
cannot be accepted; its contested clusters are exactly those that birth, merge,
or split acts upon.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Agentic consensus $k$-means for motif clustering}
\label{alg:cluster}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require motifs $M=\{m_i\}_{i=1}^N$; replicates $R$; seed count $K_0$;
         thresholds $s^\star,\varphi_{\mathrm{merge}},q_{\mathrm{split}},
         c_{\mathrm{th}},s_{\min},\varepsilon,\sigma_{\min}$
\State $P \gets \Gamma(\text{stratified sample of } M)$
       \Comment{seed $K_0$ prototypes (LLM)}
\Repeat
  \State cast $R$ votes $a_r(i)$ for every $m_i$ against $P$
         \Comment{E-step: assign (LLM, parallel)}
  \State $d(i)\gets\arg\max_k n_{i,k};\quad s_i\gets n_{i,d(i)}/R$
         \Comment{tally (code)}
  \ForAll{clusters $k$ with changed membership}
     \State $p_k^{\mathrm{old}}\gets p_k;\quad p_k \gets \Gamma(\{m_i:d(i)=k\})$
            \Comment{M-step: update centroid (LLM)}
     \State $\sigma_k\gets \textsc{SameArchetype}(p_k^{\mathrm{old}},p_k)$
            \Comment{prototype-shift test (LLM)}
  \EndFor
  \State birth new prototypes from recurring outliers
         \Comment{grow $K$ (LLM)}
  \State merge $\{A,B:\varphi(A,B)\!\ge\!\varphi_{\mathrm{merge}}\}$;
         split clusters with $q_A\!>\!q_{\mathrm{split}}$ via
         co-association \Comment{adapt $K$ (code decides, LLM names)}
  \State $\bar s_t\gets\tfrac1N\sum_i s_i;\quad
         \rho_t\gets 1-\mathrm{ARI}(d_t,d_{t-1});\quad
         \Sigma_t\gets\tfrac1K\sum_k\sigma_k$ \Comment{code}
\Until{$\bar s_t\!\ge\!s_{\min}$, $\rho_t\!<\!\varepsilon$, and
       $\Sigma_t\!\ge\!\sigma_{\min}$ (no structural change) for two rounds}
\State \Return clusters $\{C_k\}$ and prototypes $P$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Geometric similarity is judged only by the LLM votes, so the procedure requires
no descriptor engineering or embedding; conversely, the LLM is never asked to
produce a number, so the quantitative criteria in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:assign}--\eqref{eq:rho} are exact and reproducible. Unlike
Euclidean $k$-means, the vote operator is stochastic and the objective is not
guaranteed to decrease monotonically; the ensemble over $R$ replicates suppresses
this variance, and convergence is enforced by the two-round stability-and-quality
criterion above.
